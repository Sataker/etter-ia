## Week 1 — Diagnosis

Reading real conversations from recent months. It is the most revealing stage and the one almost everyone wants to skip: what a company believes customers ask rarely matches what they actually ask. This produces the list of questions carrying the volume.

**You provide:** an export of service history and read access to the systems involved.

## Week 2 — Knowledge base

Official answers get written and structured. Where no official answer exists, it gets defined now — and that usually creates value beyond automation, because it standardises what the human team says too.

**You provide:** validation of the answers. Nothing goes live without approval.

## Week 3 — Integration

The agent gets connected to what it needs to consult: calendar, CRM, tracking, stock. The rule here is conservative — it reads a lot and writes little, and every action that changes data requires confirmation until it proves stable.

## Week 4 — Assisted operation

The agent starts handling real conversations, monitored. Every conversation it gets wrong becomes a tuning task. By the end of the week it operates alone on what has been validated and hands over the rest.

## What causes delays

Access to a system that depends on a third party, an official answer nobody can settle, and scope that grows midway ("while we're here, it could also..."). All three are manageable in week one and expensive in week four.
