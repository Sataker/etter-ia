## The seven questions

**1. Do you use the official WhatsApp API?**
If the answer involves scanning a QR code from a phone, it is an unofficial solution and the number is at risk of a ban. It is the fastest filter there is.

**2. Do I keep the code and the data?**
If you cannot leave with what was built, you did not buy a system — you rented a dependency. That can be acceptable, as long as it is clear beforehand rather than afterwards.

**3. What happens when the agent gets something wrong?**
It will. The real question is who fixes it, how fast, and whether that is included. A vendor who treats the project as a one-off delivery and disappears is the most common failure pattern in this category.

**4. Show me a case with numbers.**
"We increased sales a lot" is not a case. "We cut no-shows by 70% in a barbershop with automatic confirmation" is. Numbers without industry context and mechanism are worth nothing either.

**5. What will AI not solve here?**
A vendor who says AI solves everything is selling, not diagnosing. Every project has a limit, and anyone who knows their own work can name it.

**6. Is the price fixed before work starts?**
Scope, timeline and price defined upfront. An open budget in automation becomes a bill that grows with every "while we're here".

**7. Have you ever turned a project down?**
Someone who never has accepts everything — including what will not work. And the project that should never have started is the most expensive of all, for both sides.

## Agency, freelancer or platform

**An off-the-shelf platform** solves a common process on a short budget. You adapt to the system, and for many companies that is enough.

**A freelancer** works well for small, closed scope. The risk is continuity: living automation needs someone available six months from now.

**An agency or dedicated professional** makes sense when there is integration with your own systems, specific business logic and a need for ongoing maintenance — which describes most operations past the early stage.

## One signal almost nobody checks

Ask the vendor to describe your operation back to you, in their own words, before proposing anything. Someone who understood the problem can. Someone who just wants to sell jumps straight to the tool.
